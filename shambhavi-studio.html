The Studio

Small batches, made by hand, one story at a time.

Every candle here begins as a question rather than a formula: what did that afternoon actually smell like? What is the scent of a dessert you order every week, a room you got married in, a city you left?

The answer gets built by nose — blended, poured, burned, adjusted, and burned again — until it stops being an approximation and starts being the thing itself. Nothing is mass-produced. Nothing is bought in and relabelled.

Coconut and soy wax, cotton wicks, IFRA-compliant fragrance, and glass worth keeping after the wax is gone.

Bespoke

A signature scent, made for one occasion.

For couples who want their wedding day held in something more lasting than a photograph — a fragrance built from the specifics of their story, poured as favours for the people who were there.

This is a slow, conversational process, taken on a few times a year and by introduction. It is not a catalogue scent with a name printed on it.

01

The conversation

An hour on where you met, where you're marrying, what the flowers are, what the season smells like where you're from. Details, not adjectives.

02

The drafts

Three interpretations, blended and sent to you to live with for a week. Scent reads differently in your own home than it does on paper.

03

The refinement

One direction chosen and adjusted until it's right — then burn-tested in the final vessel, because a blend that works in the bottle can behave differently in wax.

04

The pour

Hand-poured, labelled, and boxed for the day. The formula stays on file, so it can be poured again for anniversaries.

Collaborations

For cafés, florists, planners and small brands.

If a place has a smell people already associate with it, that smell can go home with them. I work with a small number of partners each year to build a scent that belongs specifically to their space or their product — sold on their counter, given to their clients, or used across an event.

What a collaboration looks like

  • A scent developed to your brand, not adapted from an existing one — built by nose against your actual product or space.
  • Samples first, always. Nothing is produced at volume until you've smelled it and said yes.
  • Small opening runs, so a first order is a low-risk test rather than a commitment.
  • Your labelling and vessel direction, or mine — whichever fits the counter it sits on.
  • Wholesale or consignment, whichever suits how you already work with makers.
